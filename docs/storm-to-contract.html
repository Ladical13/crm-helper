Project One Roofing Build Plan · Rev 1 · Sept 2026

Storm to Contract

Replacing HailTrace is the small half. The half that matters is the join it can never do — the swath against our customer list.

01

The data decision

The canvasser's hail engine cannot replace HailTrace, and no amount of polish changes that. It is built on the wrong data product.

What we have

NOAA SPC storm reports. Human-called-in points. A spotter or homeowner saw hail and phoned it in. Sparse, and biased toward where people are and who bothered. A subdivision can get shelled at 2am and produce zero reports.

What HailTrace has

MRMS MESH. NOAA merges the whole NEXRAD network with satellite, surface obs and lightning into one continuous grid and estimates max hail size at every cell — whether or not a human was standing there.

SPC answers “did anybody report hail near this address?”
MESH answers “what size hail did radar estimate over this roof, on this date.”
Only the second one closes a homeowner.

MESH is free public NOAA data. Real-time at mrms.ncep.noaa.gov/2D/MESH_Max_1440min/ as GRIB2; archived back to October 2014 on Iowa State's MTArchive, and on AWS Open Data since 2020. That archive depth is exactly the hail history a roofer wants, and it is the same product our paid vendor resells.

02

Don't build it to save the subscription

Replacing a vendor to cut a bill is the weakest reason to build anything, and we will not beat a weather company at weather. Keep paying them through a full season.

Build it because HailTrace cannot do the join. It cannot say which of our Roof Care Plan subscribers, past customers, open estimates and dead 2023 leads sit inside last night's swath. It cannot hand a canvasser a route, fire storm-response, or write a lead. That join needs our CRM, which is exactly why it is unbuyable — and why it is the moat.

Architecturally that means hail stops being a canvasser feature. Today the whole pipeline lives at canvasser/app.py:354-523 and Nimbus reaches in by pulling a module out of sys.modules by name. A company's primary data product cannot live inside the door-knocking app. It becomes hail/, and all four tools read it.

03

The workflow

0ALWAYS ON

Quiet-weather ingest hail/

MESH ingest runs nightly whether or not anything happened, maintaining the Colorado swath archive back to 2014. Every address we own carries a current hail-exposure score.

This is what makes Phase 2 instant instead of the 60-second CSV scan reps wait through today.

1T+0 → T+6h

Detection, with nobody watching hail/ storm-scout

Polls MESH_Max_1440min through severe season. Any cell in the service area over 1.0″ opens a storm_event: date, polygonized swath, max size, area. Storm-scout fires itself.

Nobody has to notice the storm. That is the whole edge over every competitor who finds out from the news.

2T+MINUTES

The join — this is the money portal/geo salescrm

Point-in-polygon the swath against everything we own, producing five ranked lists:

  • Roof Care Plan subscribers — contractual obligation, contacted first, always
  • Past customers — warranty posture, goodwill, the referral ask
  • Open pipeline leads — the storm just changed their conversation
  • Lost estimates from prior years — the reason they said no may have expired
  • Cold addresses inside the polygon — canvassing territory

HailTrace stops before line one.

3T+6h → T+24h

Mobilization storm-response

The polygon divides into rep zones by address density and drive time. Drafts generate per list, plus GBP and social. Zones push to each rep's canvasser.

Luke approves. Nothing auto-sends.

4T+24h → 2 WEEKS

The door canvasser

The rep opens to their zone, swath rendered underneath, addresses colour-coded by relationship — RCP member, past customer, open lead, cold. Two things become possible that aren't today:

  • Instant hail history for that exact address, from the local archive
  • The rep texts the homeowner their storm report from the porch — our first customer-facing artifact at the door, and the strongest opener we own

Pin type auto-creates the CRM lead with its cadence. No second button to forget.

5DAYS

Inspection → estimate salescrm estimator

Photos attach to the customer, not the estimate — the photo exists before an estimate does, and must survive one being marked lost. The estimate carries the storm_event_id and the proposal cites it.

6AT SIGNATURE

Close the loop portal/funnel

Signature pushes to The Den — already built. What's missing: the pin on the map turns gold. The rep watches the door they knocked in June become a contract.

That loop is the motivational engine of the whole tool and it does not exist today.

7SEASONS

The compounding asset unit-economics

Every storm gets an outcome record: doors → leads → contracts → revenue. After two seasons we know which hail size actually converts, how many days after a storm the window closes, which zips convert, and true cost per contract by storm.

HailTrace can never build this, because they don't have our outcomes.

05

What could make this wrong

Representation risk

MESH is an estimate and is known to run high and low. The moment we show a homeowner “your roof took 1.75″,” that is a representation we are making. It needs a source note travelling with it — the same discipline the estimator's fastening table already carries.

Claim window vs. lookback

Our current default lookback is 1,825 days. Colorado gives a homeowner one year from date of loss to notify the carrier (CRS 10-4-110.8). Most of what the tool proudly returns is not claimable — it is retail sales evidence, and the tool does not distinguish. A rep citing 2022 hail is walking into a bad adjuster conversation.

Unverified from here

The dev sandbox proxy blocks both mrms.ncep.noaa.gov and mtarchive.geol.iastate.edu, so file cadence, size and retention are unmeasured. A 10-minute spike from a box with open egress must happen before anyone commits to a timeline for item 2.

Parallel-run discipline

Run HailTrace alongside for one full season and compare our swaths to theirs on the same storms. It is the only way to trust our own map enough to put it in front of a homeowner — and cheap next to being wrong in a driveway.
